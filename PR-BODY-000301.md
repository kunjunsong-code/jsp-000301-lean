### Mathematical solution

The problem asks: if two consecutive positive integers are both **powerful** (every prime divisor appears with exponent ≥ 2), must at least one be a perfect square?

Solomon W. Golomb answered **No** in 1970 with the explicit counterexample:

\begin{aligned}
12167 &= 23^3 \\
12168 &= 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 13^2
\end{aligned}

Both are powerful (every prime factor has exponent ≥ 2), neither is a perfect square ($\lfloor\sqrt{12167}\rfloor = 110$, $\lfloor\sqrt{12168}\rfloor = 110$), and they are consecutive ( = 12167 + 1$). This refutes the universally-quantified claim.

**Reference**: [Golomb, S. W. (1970). "Powerful numbers". *American Mathematical Monthly* 77(8): 848–852.](https://doi.org/10.2307/2317020) — catalogued as [Go70] in the awards problem bank.
